# 1. Three operating modes\
\
The Virtual Laboratory should have **three main modes**.\
\
### 👨‍🏫 Teacher Demonstration\
\
A teacher can project/open an experiment on a computer, smart board, tablet or classroom screen.\
\
The teacher controls the experiment and demonstrates it to the class.\
\
### 👩‍🎓 Learner Experiment\
\
Learners **Grade 5 and above** can independently enter an experiment and manipulate the virtual equipment.\
\
### 📝 Assigned Experiment\
\
The teacher assigns an experiment to a particular:\
\
- Grade\
- Stream\
- Class\
- Group\
- Individual learner\
\
The learner conducts it and submits the results.\
\
This gives you a very important distinction:\
\
\u003e **Demonstrate → Explore → Experiment → Record → Submit → Assess**\
\

# 2. Grade access\
\
I would configure access from **Grade 5 upward**, but not give every grade the same laboratory complexity.\
\
| Level | Experience |\
|---|---|\
| Grade 5 | Guided, simple experiments |\
| Grade 6 | Guided + basic measurements |\
| Grade 7 | More independent experimentation |\
| Grade 8 | Independent + data analysis |\
| Grade 9 | Advanced experimentation + graphs |\
| Grade 10–12 | More sophisticated simulations, calculations and investigations |\
\
The same simulation engine can therefore serve the whole school while the **difficulty, instructions and assessment change by grade**.\

# 19. The most important design principle\
\
I would make the Virtual Laboratory operate on **one simulation engine but two interfaces**:\
\
### Teacher interface\
\
**DEMONSTRATE**\
\
Large controls, explanations, annotations, answer reveal, classroom presentation.\
\
### Learner interface\
\
**DISCOVER \u0026 CONDUCT**\
\
Interactive equipment, measurements, investigation, notebook and assessment.\
\
Both use the **same underlying experiment**.\
\
That means a teacher could demonstrate **Ohm's Law in the morning**, and then assign the exact same simulation to Grade 8 learners to conduct independently.\
\

# 20. Recommended first release\
\
Rather than immediately trying to reproduce hundreds of PhET-style simulations, I recommend building the Resources module around **10 highly polished experiments first**:\
\
### Grade 5–6\
1. Parts of a Plant\
2. Seed Germination\
3. States of Matter\
4. Simple Machines\
5. Simple Electric Circuit\
\
### Grade 7–9\
6. Series \u0026 Parallel Circuits\
7. Ohm's Law\
8. Reflection \u0026 Refraction\
9. Acids \u0026 Bases\
10. Separation of Mixtures\
\
Once the underlying engine works properly, adding experiment number 11, 12, 50 or 100 becomes progressively easier.\
\
**The final Resources architecture should therefore be:**\
\
```text\
                    EDUSMART\

                  📚 RESOURCES\
